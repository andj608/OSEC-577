You are the pharmacist at a community pharmacy. A 32-year-old man comes in asking for antihistamines for a rash that appeared 5 days ago. He has recently started a new medication. Conduct an assessment to determine the likely cause and urgency of management.

⚠ Note: Drug rashes range from mild morbilliform rashes (common, self-limiting) to life-threatening Stevens-Johnson Syndrome (SJS). The pharmacist must screen for ALARM features of severe cutaneous adverse reactions (SCAR): mucosal involvement, blistering, skin detachment, fever, facial oedema. If any ALARM features present — do not sell antihistamines; send to ED immediately.
